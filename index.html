How do we stay safe when a hurricane comes?

A hurricane is a giant, spinning storm. It can bring very strong wind, lots of rain, and flooding.

The good news: we can see hurricanes coming days ahead of time. That gives people time to get ready. Today you will learn what a hurricane is, what it looks and feels like, how to stay safe, and what to do after it is over.

Part 1 of 7

What is a hurricane?

A hurricane is a huge storm that spins. It starts over warm ocean water.

Warm water is the fuel. The warm ocean heats the air above it. The warm, wet air rises up and makes big thunderstorms.

Earth’s spin makes it turn. The storms start to spin around a center. When the wind reaches 74 miles per hour, it is called a hurricane.

Land and cool water make it weaker. Without warm water, the storm runs out of fuel.

What will I see, hear, and feel?

A hurricane does not hit all at once. It gets worse over many hours. Knowing what is coming helps you stay calm and make good choices.

Watch the water rise

The ocean can rise and flood the land. This is called storm surge. It is the most dangerous part of most hurricanes. Start on a normal day. Then try stronger storms.

Hurricanes get a category from 1 to 5. A bigger number means stronger wind. But even a Category 1 can bring dangerous water.

The calm in the middle

The center of a hurricane is called the eye. When the eye passes over you, the wind stops and it may get sunny. The storm is not over. The other side of the storm is coming.

Part 3 of 7

Watch or warning?

Weather experts send out alerts before a hurricane. There are two kinds. Each one tells you what to do.

⚠️ Hurricane watch

The storm might come. It could arrive in about 2 days.

Get ready: pack your kit, charge phones, make a plan.

🚨 Hurricane warning

The storm is coming. It will arrive in about 1 and a half days.

Act now: if leaders say leave, leave right away.

Memory tip: Watch = Wait and get ready. Warning = Work fast and go.

    Get ready and stay safe

    Remember this rule: Run from water. Hide from wind.

    If water could flood your home, you need to leave (this is called evacuating). If your home is safe from water, you can stay inside, away from windows.

    After the storm: stay safe and recover

    When the wind stops, the danger is not over. Many people get hurt after a hurricane. Wait until adults or leaders say it is safe to go outside.
